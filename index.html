SETTINGS

FX + GLUE per scene: each scene stores and recalls the reverb and the compressor. Global: they stay as you set them, whatever you recall. Morph: how long a scene or preset takes to arrive from what plays now; OFF = at once (a 15 ms glide). Rec loop: Free, each line its own length; Stretch, one cycle as long as the longest line; Fixed, one cycle as long as line 1. Rec loop smoothing: how softly the lines move a control from one recorded value to the next, and from a loop's end back to its start; OFF keeps the steps as recorded.

INFOWhat it is

SKREWELL is a self-playing drone and texture instrument: eight channels that modulate each other, feed back through their own delays and never quite settle. You don't play notes. You shape a system and listen to what it does. It is a rebuild of Skrewell, an ensemble for Native Instruments Reaktor 5 by Alexander Potekhin aka Lazyfish, with all 89 presets of its first bank, made first as firmware for the M-VAVE FM-1 and then as this page.

THE PANEL, TOP TO BOTTOM

PLAYStarts and stops the sound (Space). While it plays, the screen stays awake.
RECRecords automation lines: your moves, not sound. Press REC and move anything; press again and that line loops from that moment, at the length you played. Press REC again for a new line while the others keep looping, up to 8. REC LOOP (settings) decides how lines relate. Free: every line keeps its own length, so they drift against each other. Stretch: the lines share one cycle, as long as the longest; a take starts where the cycle is, one that runs past the end stretches it, and shorter lines hold their last values until it comes round. Fixed: line 1 sets the cycle; later takes record round and round inside it, the new moves replacing their own from the round before. Only the controls you touch are recorded. A control an earlier line already moves is taken over by the new line only over the stretch you recorded, from your first move of it until REC stops; outside that stretch the earlier line keeps playing it. Your hand always wins while it moves a control. Hold REC 2 s to clear every line (a saved scene keeps its own until you save over it). A scene saves its lines and how they sit against each other; recalling it starts line 1 from its beginning and the others in the same relation.
SCOPEEach channel's left lever drawn against its right, eight figures over each other. Tap it (or F) and the controls fade away; tap again (or Esc) to bring them back.
HEADERThe preset's number and name, the scene (S1–S8) and the mode. Messages appear in the same row for a moment.
SCENEEight scenes of the current preset. Tap one to recall it: it morphs in from what plays now (see MORPH in settings). A filled cell has a scene stored; an empty one is free.
SAVEArms saving: the eight scene cells light up. Tap one to store what plays now there, loop included. Whatever was in it is overwritten, without asking. Tap SAVE again to cancel.
MODEImplant Quad, Xung Tekh or Subtotal (Z, X, C): three ways of building a lever, below.
FUNCTION‹ › step through the functions (or 1–8); the faders show the one you pick. Subtotal has no filter, so it has six.
FADERSEight faders, one per channel, for the chosen function. Drag with as many fingers as you like; each finger moves its own fader. ← → pick a channel and ↑ ↓ move it from the keyboard (Shift for bigger steps).
DRAW · WRAP · RANDHow a drag edits (A, S, D). DRAW sets the fader you touch. WRAP moves all eight together, folding back at the ends so their pattern is kept. RAND scatters all eight each time you touch.
RANDOMISE ALLNew positions for all the faders, in every function at once. Nothing else changes: mode, knobs, FX and GLUE stay where they are. On a keyboard: the ? key.
OSC · FILTER · DELAYBend all eight values of their function at once: the whole system higher or lower, brighter or darker, shorter or longer, keeping the faders' shape.
FLOWMoves the preset's hidden settings between their two stored extremes: modulation depth, resonance, smoothing and the normaliser's floor (below). The same position does something different in every preset.
VOLUMEThe listening level of this page. It is not part of a scene or a preset.
PRESETSThe 89 presets of the original's bank, in its order and with its names, in 6 octaves of 16 (the numbers 1–6 above them). A preset is an instrument: its hidden settings are fixed and can't be changed, here or in the original. What you change and save are its scenes. Tap a preset and it plays the scene you last played in it (scene 1 the first time). Hold a preset 1 s and every control returns to the original's settings; the header shows ORIG, and SAVE then a scene keeps them. E1–E14 beside the number is its engine (below).
REVERBSIZE, DECAY, CUT and MIX. CUT is one slider with two ends: the low cut and the high cut of what goes back into the mix. SEND adds the reverb beside the dry sound; INSERT puts the whole sound through it, and MIX sets the balance.
GLUEA bus compressor on the sum: THRESHOLD, MAKEUP, RELEASE and DRY/WET, with the ratio beside the title. The meter shows how much it is pressing down.

Everything you save stays in this browser (or in your account, when the page runs as a Claude artifact). Settings, the current preset and scene, and the volume come back when you open the page again.

SIGNAL FLOW

EIGHT CHANNELS, SIXTEEN LEVERS

Each fader sets one channel, and a channel is two levers, left and right, built the same. What makes them differ is who modulates them: a lever's pitch and level follow a lever on the other side, picked per channel by FREQ MOD and AMP MOD (solid: right modulating left; dashed: left modulating right). The left levers make the left output, the right levers the right.

Each of the eight channels has a left and a right side: sixteen levers. A lever is an oscillator, a filter, a delay line with feedback, and a normaliser that holds its level steady however hard the feedback pushes. Each lever's pitch and level are modulated by a lever on the other side, so small changes travel around the whole system: it drifts, locks, breaks apart and recombines. The scope draws each channel's left against its right.

THE THREE MODES

IQImplant Quad: square oscillators; a high-pass and a low-pass inside the feedback loop.
XTXung Tekh: square oscillators; one filter, low-pass to band-pass to high-pass, on the oscillator before the loop.
STSubtotal: rounder oscillators, no filter; each lever listens to the sum of all the others.

THE EIGHT FUNCTIONS

Each function sets one value per channel: OSC FREQ (pitch), FREQ MOD and AMP MOD (which lever modulates), OSC AMP (level), the two filter functions, DELAY (1 ms to 3 s) and FEEDBACK. OSC, FILTER and DELAY bend all eight values at once; FLOW moves the hidden settings between two extremes stored in each preset.

PRESETS AND SCENES

In the original, each of Skrewell's snapshots is more than its faders and knobs. Each one also carries settings that have no control on the panel, here or in the original: they were set by the preset's designer and travel with it. That is why the same fader positions can sound completely different from one preset to the next, and why FLOW does something different in each. So here a preset is that instrument, its hidden settings locked, and a scene is a state of its panel: mode, faders, knobs, FX and GLUE, and its loop. Think of scenes as snapshots that belong to one instrument. Each preset holds 8 scenes; scene 1 comes with the preset and is yours to change. Moving between scenes of one preset morphs smoothly, because they share the same hidden settings; moving to another preset changes the instrument underneath.

RANGEWhat 0 and 100 mean on the faders: the lowest and highest pitch for OSC FREQ, the lowest and highest frequency for the filters (high-pass, low-pass, cutoff), the shortest and longest DELAY.
FLOWFive settings, each stored twice (FLOW at 0 and at 100) and separately for each mode: FM depth, AM depth, filter resonance, smoothing (how fast pitch, delay and level respond) and the normaliser's floor. FLOW blends between the two; changing mode switches to that mode's set.
LEVELThe preset's output level: the original's OUT knob (−36 to +18 dB). It was on Reaktor's panel; here it isn't, so it travels with the preset.

The 89 originals are kept exactly as designed, in their order and with their names: each one is a composed work. Underneath, they share 14 engines, sets of hidden ranges and FLOW settings: 56 presets use the first, 16 the second, 5 the third, and the rest are smaller families or stand alone. The small E1–E14 beside each preset's number shows its engine, so you can see which pieces are written for the same instrument.
