INFOWhat it is

SKREWELL is eight voices that push each other. You don't play notes on it: you decide who pushes whom, and how hard, and listen to what they do together. It is a rebuild of Skrewell, an ensemble for Native Instruments Reaktor 5 by Alexander Potekhin aka Lazyfish, with all 89 presets of its first bank, made first as firmware for the M-VAVE FM-1 and then as this app.

THE WORDS

CHANNELOne of the eight voices: an oscillator, a filter and a delay loop.
SIDEEvery channel runs twice, as a left and a right side. Each side is pushed by a channel of the other side, so the two halves of a voice pull on each other. That is the stereo, and what the scope draws.
FUNCTIONOne part that every channel has, such as its pitch or its delay time. There are eight (six in Subtotal); ‹ › steps through them.
FADEROne function of one channel. The eight faders show one function across all eight channels; fader 1 across all the functions is everything about channel 1.
MODEHow the channels are wired. Implant Quad: square-wave oscillators, a high-pass and a low-pass filter inside the delay loop. Xung Tekh: square-wave oscillators, one filter (low-pass to band-pass to high-pass) before the loop. Subtotal: rounder oscillators, no filter, and each channel is pushed by the sum of all the others.
NORMALIZERHolds every voice at a steady level however hard it is pushed, so the sound never runs away or dies out. It has no control of its own.
PRESETOne of Lazyfish's 89 instruments. Its hidden settings never change.
SCENEYour state of the panel inside a preset. Each preset has eight.
AUTOMATION LINEA recording of your moves that loops.

ONE CHANNEL

WHAT EACH CONTROL DOES

1 OSC FREQThe channel's basic pitch, within the range the preset allows.
2 FREQ MODWhich channel of the other side bends this one's pitch, at full audio speed: from channel 1 at the bottom to channel 8 at the top, blending neighbours in between. In Subtotal: how strongly the sum of the others bends it.
3 OSC AMPHow much of the oscillator goes into the loop. With feedback up, the normalizer holds the level, so it changes the balance of oscillator and delay more than the volume. At zero the voice stops.
4 AMP MODWhich channel of the other side moves this one's level, chosen like FREQ MOD. In Subtotal: how strongly the sum of the others moves it.
5 HIGH PASS · FILTER CUTOFFImplant Quad: the high-pass filter. Xung Tekh: where its filter cuts.
6 LOW-PASS · LOW-BAND-HIGHImplant Quad: the low-pass filter, which carries the high-pass along with it. Xung Tekh: the filter type, from low-pass through band-pass to high-pass.
7 DELAYThe delay time, from about 1 ms to 3 s within the preset's range. With feedback up, short times ring at a pitch of their own; long times bring back what the voice did a moment ago.
8 FEEDBACKHow much of the channel's output returns to its input. High, the voice feeds itself and rings on.
OSC · FILTER · DELAYEach changes how its function's faders are read, not the faders themselves. In the middle, a fader's position sets its value directly; turned down, most positions give lower values (lower pitch, darker filters, shorter delays); turned up, higher. A fader at 0 or 100 stays at the end of the range. FILTER acts on both filter functions.
FLOWMoves five hidden settings between two points the preset's designer chose: pitch push depth, level push depth, filter resonance, glide speed and how far the normalizer lifts quiet sounds. In almost every preset, turning it up makes the voices pull harder on each other.

READING THE SCOPE

Each channel draws its left side against its right: the left moves the dot sideways, the right up and down. The shape shows how the two sides relate.

LINEBoth sides play the same thing.
SLANTThe same, one side louder.
OVALThe same pitch, one side later.
TURNINGAlmost the same pitch; you often hear it as slow beating.
KNOTThe pitches in a simple ratio, such as an octave or a fifth.
CLOUDNothing in common: noise, the system breaking apart.

THE PANEL, TOP TO BOTTOM

PLAYStarts and stops the sound (Space). While it plays, the screen stays awake.
RECRecords automation lines: your moves, not sound.
  1. Press REC and move anything: faders, knobs, mode, FX, GLUE.
  2. Press REC again: that line loops from that moment. The lines already playing keep playing.
  3. Press REC again for the next line, up to 8.
  4. Hold REC 2 s to clear every line.
Only what you touch is recorded. A newer line takes a control over only for the stretch it recorded; outside it, the earlier line keeps playing it. Your hand wins while it moves. How lines relate is set by REC LOOP in settings: Free (each its own length), Stretch (one cycle, as long as the longest line) or Fixed (line 1 sets the cycle). A scene stores its lines.
SCOPEEach channel's left side drawn against its right, eight figures over each other. Tap it (or F) and the controls fade away; tap again (or Esc) to bring them back.
HEADERThe preset's number and name, the scene (S1–S8) and the mode. Messages appear in the same row for a moment.
SCENEEight scenes of the current preset. Tap one to recall it: it morphs in from what plays now (see MORPH in settings). A filled cell has a scene stored; an empty one is free.
SAVEArms saving: the eight scene cells light up. Tap one to store what plays now there, automation lines included. Whatever was in it is overwritten, without asking. Tap SAVE again to cancel.
MODEImplant Quad, Xung Tekh or Subtotal (Z, X, C).
FUNCTION‹ › step through the functions (or 1–8); the faders show the one you pick. Subtotal has no filter, so it has six.
FADERSEight faders, one per channel, for the chosen function. Drag with as many fingers as you like; each finger moves its own fader. ← → pick a channel and ↑ ↓ move it from the keyboard (Shift for bigger steps).
DRAW · WRAP · RANDHow a drag edits (A, S, D). DRAW sets the fader you touch. WRAP moves all eight together, folding back at the ends so their pattern is kept. RAND scatters all eight each time you touch.
RANDOMISE ALLNew positions for all the faders, in every function at once. Nothing else changes: mode, knobs, FX and GLUE stay where they are. On a keyboard: the ? key.
VOLUMEThe listening level of the app. It is not part of a scene or a preset.
PRESETSThe 89 presets, in 6 octaves of 16 (the numbers 1–6 above them). Tap one and it plays the scene you last played in it. Hold one 1 s and every control returns to the original; the header shows ORIG, and SAVE then a scene keeps it. E1–E14 beside the number is its engine (below).
REVERBSIZE, DECAY, CUT and MIX. CUT is one slider with two ends: the low cut and the high cut of what goes back into the mix. SEND adds the reverb beside the dry sound; INSERT puts the whole sound through it, and MIX sets the balance.
GLUEA bus compressor on the sum: THRESHOLD, MAKEUP, RELEASE and DRY/WET, with the ratio beside the title. The meter shows how much it is pressing down.

Everything you save stays in this browser. Settings, the current preset and scene, and the volume come back when you open the app again.

PRESETS AND SCENES

Each preset carries settings no control reaches, set by its designer. That is why the same faders sound different in every preset, and why FLOW does something different in each:

RANGEWhat 0 and 100 mean on the faders: the lowest and highest pitch, filter frequencies and delay times.
FLOWFive settings, stored for FLOW at 0 and at 100, separately for each mode.
LEVELThe preset's output level (the original's OUT knob).

Moving between scenes of one preset morphs smoothly, because they share these settings; another preset changes the instrument underneath. The 89 presets share 14 engines (sets of these settings): 56 use the first, 16 the second, 5 the third, the rest are small families or stand alone. E1–E14 beside each preset shows its engine.
